Cache Invalidation & Durable Recall

Durable recall requires storing the source, the reader's judgment, and the evidence behind each connection. “Memory” is useful when previous concerns can resurface alongside the exact observations that support them. A recalled claim should remain attached to its source so that the reader can inspect how an answer was formed.

A second paragraph explains cache invalidation: changing the underlying reading should trigger a new analysis when the previous interpretation no longer applies. Preserve ©, ©, —, <T>, and & entities as readable text. Keep this paragraph separate from the first, even when the HTML has no meaningful source whitespace.

A concrete example

An analyst may save a paper about governed metrics, disagree with one assumption, and return to it after a new source challenges the same idea. The system should preserve this history as explicit annotations instead of silently interpreting repeated appearances as endorsement.
